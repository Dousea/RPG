# The city

## Calder

Calder is a working river city of a few hundred thousand people, built where
the Sallow River widens on its way to the sea. It has never been pretty and
has never pretended to be. The money is on the west bank: downtown's towers,
the university on the hill, the old merchants' houses turned into law
offices, and the glass campus of Velloran Biologics. The work is on the east
bank: the container terminal, the rail yards, the warehouses, and the
neighbourhoods that grew up to house the people who did the lifting. People
from the west bank say they are going "over the river" when they mean the east
bank, and they rarely go.

The game begins in early October. Calder's autumn is grey and damp, with river
fog at dawn that burns off by mid-morning, and the first frosts usually come
at the end of the month.

## The Sallow and its bridges

The Sallow is wide and brown at Calder, cold in October and too fast for
anyone to swim across and expect to arrive. Barges, the river ferry and the
harbour pilot boats crossed it all day before the outbreak.

Three bridges join the east bank to the rest of the city:

- **The Ferry Street Bridge**, the oldest, a narrow iron truss bridge with a
  walkway on each side, built where the ferry used to land. Ashby's own
  bridge: people walk it to work downtown.
- **The Harbour Bridge**, a long concrete highway bridge downriver, carrying
  the ring road over the terminal's mouth.
- **The rail bridge**, upriver, carrying freight to the yards. No walkway;
  people cross it anyway.

The Blue Line subway runs under the river from Ashby station to downtown, the
fastest way across for anyone without a car.

When the bridges close, the east bank becomes an island. That is the shape of
the game's world: everything the player can reach is on one side of the
water, and everything they hear about is on the other.

## The port

Calder's container terminal sits downriver of Ashby, its blue cranes visible
from any rooftop in the neighbourhood. For a century the docks fed the east
bank; automation cut the jobs to a few hundred, and most of the old dockworkers
now do something else or nothing. Marcus, the super in the player's building,
worked the terminal for twenty years.

In the last week of September, the container ship *Merrow Star* was refused a
berth and held at anchor off the terminal after several of her crew reported
sick with fever. The news filmed her every day: a grey hull in the fog, men in
white suits on the deck, a patrol boat circling. When the fever reached
Calder's hospital a few days later, the ship was the story everyone already
had. See [the truth](the-truth.md) for why it was wrong.

## St. Agnes

St. Agnes is Calder's big public hospital, downtown on the west bank: a
Victorian brick block with a new glass emergency wing bolted to its side. The
first fever patients arrived there at the end of September, confused and
burning, and some of them bit the staff who held them down. By the time the
news named it the "Ashby fever", ambulances were queuing round the block and
the police were on its doors.

The day after the sirens, smoke started rising from downtown near St. Agnes
and did not stop. Nobody on the radio has said what happened there.

## Calder Stadium

Calder Stadium is home to the Calder Rivermen, a football team that has not
won anything in living memory and is loved for it. It stands on the west bank
with a scoreboard that reads VELLORAN across the top; Velloran Biologics paid
for the new roof.

On day 4 the radio named the stadium as the evacuation point. Buses gathered
at Tanner Park in Ashby and crossed the bridges for two days. People who got
there texted home at first: long queues, tents on the pitch, soldiers checking
every arm for bites. When the bridges closed, the texts stopped. The
floodlights kept burning across the river every night until the power went,
and then the west bank was dark too. What happened inside is not part of this
story, and no source in the game says.

## Velloran Biologics

Velloran is Calder's biggest private employer after the port: a drug company
with a glass campus on the west bank, a foundation that funds the university's
medical school, and its name on the stadium. Its public face is clean,
civic-minded and dull. Its experimental nerve-repair drug, VX-7, was in a
paid trial at the Ashby Community Clinic. See [the truth](the-truth.md).

## The people in charge

**Mayor Helen Strand** spent the week before the sirens calling the fever a
bad flu season, telling people not to crowd the hospitals and promising that
the ship was contained. After the sirens she never spoke again; a colonel
whose name was never given read the announcements in her place.

**The police** were stretched thin before anything started: their cars parked
outside St. Agnes, and the Dock Road station sent nobody for break-ins. After
the evacuation call, nobody answered at Dock Road at all. The officers who
kept working went to the bridges with the army.

**The army** arrived with the evacuation: trucks escorting the buses, soldiers
in masks waving cars across the bridges and taking temperatures on the far
side. When the cordon came, the same soldiers turned around. They hold the
bridges behind trucks and wire, they talk to no one, and at night their
gunfire carries across Ashby in short bursts. On the third night of the
cordon the Ferry Street Bridge burned in the middle, and the army let it.

**Helicopters** crossed the river low at dawn and dusk in the first days of
the cordon. None ever landed on the east bank. After about five days they
stopped coming.

## The radio, the TV and the phones

**River FM**, Calder's talk station, ran call-in shows about the fever between
the traffic news and the football. When the sirens started, it became the
emergency broadcast: stay indoors, lock the doors, keep away from anyone
feverish. On day 4 it named the evacuation point; on day 6 it called the
evacuation "suspended". On day 9 the live voice stopped mid-sentence and a
recording took over, one message on a loop: stay indoors, wait for
instructions. It is still playing.

**The TV** ran the same footage on a loop in the first days: the ship at
anchor, the queue at St. Agnes, the mayor at her podium. Two days after the
sirens, the channels went to test cards one by one, and the internet loads
nothing but error pages.

**The phones** jammed the moment the sirens started, with everyone in the
city calling family at once; only some texts got through. After the cordon,
the east bank lost its signal entirely.

**The power** failed across the city on the evening of day 14, all at once.
**The water** stopped on day 21: the pipes knocked, the taps gave a last spit
of air, and that was the end of it. Some buildings have roof tanks.
