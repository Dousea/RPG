# The truth

Spoilers: this is what the player can find out, and what the narrator must
never say before they do.

## The public story

The news blames the *Merrow Star*, a container ship held at anchor off the
terminal in the last week of September after some of her crew reported sick.
She was the first thing on every screen when the fever reached St. Agnes, so
she became the story. Most people believe it, and so does Jen. The mayor
promised the ship was contained, which only made people surer that it was the
source.

It is wrong. The crew had a common flu, and not one of them has turned.

## What happened

Velloran Biologics developed **VX-7**, an experimental drug meant to make
damaged nerves heal and keep firing: for spinal injuries, for nerve damage,
for the hands of old men who can't hold a cup. It needed human volunteers, and
volunteers are cheapest where people need the money. Velloran funded the
Ashby Community Clinic for a year, and in the summer the clinic began a paid
study: an injection in the crook of the arm every week, a cheque on payday.
Most of the volunteers were from Ashby.

VX-7 worked too well. A body full of it keeps its nerves firing after the
heart stops, and what is left gets up and walks. Before death it brings a
fever that won't break, grey skin, confusion and an urge to bite, and a bite
passes it on. That is why it spread from Ashby's study volunteers to the staff
who held them down at St. Agnes, and from there to the city.

The first fever patients at St. Agnes were study volunteers. When they started
coming in, Dr. Imrie called Velloran. Velloran sent the clinic a memo telling
it to stop enrolling and tell no one, a week before the sirens. Velloran knew.

This truth is never canon. The narrator knows nothing about why the dead rise
until the player does.

## How the player finds out

The truth comes in pieces, each from a different person or place, and each
piece is a flag the engine discovers
([world-lore](../mechanics/world-lore.md)):

1. **What it is** (`apocalypse_cause`), from Jen once the outbreak has
   started: the news calls it the Ashby fever, bites spread it, and the dead
   get back up. The ship story comes with it.
2. **The bandages** (`clinic_odd`), from Jen at any time: the clinic's paid
   study volunteers came in on payday with bandaged arms, and lately looked
   grey.
3. **The nurse's texts** (`hospital_texts`), from Jen after the sirens: the
   first patients at St. Agnes all had the same bandage.
4. **The ship was fine** (`ship_doubt`), from Marcus once the player knows the
   ship story: its crew had a flu and nobody turned.
5. **The neighbours** (`novaks_left`), from Marcus: Tomas Novak's fever, a week
   before the sirens.
6. **Danny** (`trial_rumour`), from Emma, who trusts the player: her brother
   took the clinic's study and disappeared.
7. **The flyers** (`study_flyers`), from Emma, later: she made the
   recruitment flyers, for a client called Velloran.
8. **The binder** (`trial_truth`): a ring binder in the clinic office's
   filing cabinet. It is the study log: VX-7, Velloran Biologics, a list of
   Ashby names, fever charts on the last pages, Dr. Imrie's own temperatures,
   and Velloran's memo in the back pocket. Taking it completes "Find Where It
   Started".

The pieces can be found in any order. The binder alone is enough to know the
truth; the others make it personal. A player who has heard about Danny or the
Novaks finds their names in the binder; one who hasn't sees only a list.

Once the binder is found, the ship story is gone for good: the narrator's
line about it becomes "The ship was a story. It started at the Ashby clinic."

## What stays unknown

- What happened at the stadium.
- What happened at St. Agnes after the smoke.
- Whether Velloran's people got out, and whether anyone outside Calder knows.
- Whether there is a cure. There isn't one in the game.

These are left open on purpose, for later stories to fill or never fill.
